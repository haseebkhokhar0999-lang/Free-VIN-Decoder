    Before starting a VIN check or VIN search, locate the 17-character identification plate stamped on the vehicle or printed on your vehicle paperwork.

    Characters 17 characters (digits 0–9 and letters A–Z, never I, O, or Q)
    Where to find Driver's-side dashboard at the base of the windshield, and the driver's door jamb label
    First digit Country or region of manufacture (for example, 1, 4, or 5 for the United States)

    VIN Decoder vs VIN Check – What's the Difference?

    A free VIN decoder translates the 17 characters assigned at the factory into build specifications—such as make, model, model year, engine, transmission, drivetrain, and assembly plant—alongside vehicle-level open safety recalls from NHTSA data.

    A VIN check (full vehicle history report) is a paid record search compiled from state DMVs, insurance carriers, and salvage auctions to uncover past title brands, odometer readings, active liens, theft filings, and reported accidents.

    What Vehicle Specs Can a VIN Reveal?

    Each free VIN lookup parses manufacturer-submitted NHTSA vPIC fields into clear factory specifications:

    • Engine: displacement (L), cylinder count, engine model, brake horsepower, and engine manufacturer.
    • Drivetrain: drive layout such as FWD, RWD, AWD, or 4WD when reported.
    • Wheelbase & Body Style: body class (sedan, coupe, SUV, pickup, van) and wheelbase/vehicle type.
    • Transmission: transmission style (automatic, manual, CVT) and number of speeds.
    • Fuel: primary fuel type, secondary fuel type, and electrification level for hybrid or EV models.
    • GVWR & Trim: Gross Vehicle Weight Rating class, series, and factory trim designation.
    • Build Plant & Country: assembly city, state, country, and manufacturer DBA names.
    • Safety Equipment: front, knee, side, curtain, and seat-cushion airbag locations plus restraint info.

    VIN FAQs

    1. Is this free VIN decoder really free?

    Yes. Every VIN lookup and recall check on this site is completely free with no account signup, no email requirement, and no paywall.

    2. How do I check a VIN with this online VIN search?

    Paste or type up to 17 characters into the VIN box at the top of this page (with an optional 4-digit model year) and click Decode VIN. Factory specs and open recalls load right below the form.

    3. Can I decode pre-1981 VINs?

    The standardized 17-character VIN format began with the 1981 model year. Vehicles built before 1981 used shorter manufacturer-specific chassis numbers that are generally not decoded in modern NHTSA vPIC records.

    4. What if my VIN appears to show the letter O, I, or Q?

    Letters I, O, and Q are never used in a valid VIN to avoid confusion with numbers. Try substituting 1 for I, 0 for O, or 9 for Q and check the plate again.

    5. How often is vehicle and recall data updated?

    Lookups query public NHTSA vPIC and Recalls records in real time as manufacturers publish updates, with data refreshed daily.

    6. Does this VIN lookup work for vehicles in Canada or Europe?

    Many Canadian-market vehicles share North American 49 CFR Part 565 VIN standards and decode cleanly. European or non-US domestic vehicles that were never certified for the US market may return partial results or no NHTSA record.

    7. Can I decode trailers, trucks, and motorcycles?

    Yes. Any US-certified motor vehicle, pickup, heavy truck, bus, trailer, or motorcycle with a 17-character VIN reported to NHTSA vPIC can be decoded here.

    8. What is a VIN validator and how does position 9 work?

    A VIN validator verifies character rules and checks position 9—the mathematical check digit defined in 49 CFR Part 565—against the rest of the VIN to catch typos or altered digits.

    9. Are VINs I search stored anywhere?

    No. Entered VINs are used solely to fetch your lookup results and are never stored.
